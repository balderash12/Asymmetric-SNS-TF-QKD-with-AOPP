# Asymmetric SNS-TF-QKD with Actively Odd-Parity Pairing (AOPP)

Numerical investigation of asymmetric sending-or-not-sending twin-field quantum key distribution (SNS-TF-QKD), focusing on the influence of channel asymmetry, signal intensities, sending probabilities, and classical post-processing through actively odd-parity pairing (AOPP) on the secret key rate.

**Repository:** [Asymmetric-SNS-TF-QKD-with-AOPP](https://github.com/balderash12/Asymmetric-SNS-TF-QKD-with-AOPP)

## Overview

Twin-field quantum key distribution (TF-QKD) is a family of measurement-device-independent quantum key distribution protocols designed to improve the achievable key rate over long optical-fibre distances. The sending-or-not-sending (SNS) variant uses signal windows in which Alice and Bob independently decide whether to send a phase-randomized coherent state or remain silent.

This repository investigates the numerical performance of SNS-TF-QKD when the two users have **asymmetric channel lengths and potentially unequal channel transmittances and protocol parameters**. It also explores the role of actively odd-parity pairing (AOPP), a classical post-processing technique used to improve the performance of SNS-TF-QKD.

The central question is:

> How does channel asymmetry influence the achievable secret key rate, and under what conditions can an asymmetric configuration outperform a symmetric one?

The notebook explores this question through numerical calculations, parameter sweeps, distance-asymmetry comparisons, and numerical optimization of protocol parameters.

## Research Objectives

The notebook is organized around the following objectives:

- **Channel asymmetry:** Investigate the effects of unequal Alice–Charlie and Bob–Charlie channel lengths.
- **Key-rate characterization:** Evaluate the dependence of the calculated key rate on channel distance and protocol parameters.
- **Symmetric versus asymmetric configurations:** Compare configurations with matched channels against configurations with unequal distances or independently selected signal parameters.
- **Parameter sensitivity:** Examine how signal intensities, sending probabilities, and system parameters influence the calculated key rate.
- **Numerical optimization:** Explore parameter choices that maximize the model's key-rate objective under specified constraints.
- **Interactive exploration:** Use notebook visualizations and widgets to examine how changes in asymmetry and selected parameters affect the results.

A key motivation is that **the effect of asymmetry is parameter-dependent**. Symmetry should not be assumed to maximize the key rate in every fixed-parameter comparison, and asymmetric configurations should not be assumed to offer a universal advantage either.

## Background: SNS-TF-QKD and AOPP

### Twin-Field Quantum Key Distribution

In a typical TF-QKD arrangement, Alice and Bob send optical signals to an intermediate measurement node, Charlie. The protocol uses interference of optical fields at this central node to establish correlations between the users' key bits.

Unlike conventional point-to-point QKD, TF-QKD can achieve a key-rate scaling associated with the square root of the end-to-end channel transmittance under appropriate protocol assumptions. This makes it particularly relevant to long-distance quantum communication.

### Sending-or-Not-Sending Protocol

In SNS-TF-QKD, Alice and Bob independently select signal and decoy windows according to their respective probability distributions.

In a signal window, a user encodes a bit by choosing between sending a phase-randomized coherent state and sending vacuum. Decoy-state statistics are used to estimate quantities needed for secure key generation.

The two users' protocol parameters need not be identical. In an asymmetric configuration, these may include:

- Channel lengths and transmittances.
- Coherent-state intensities.
- Probabilities of selecting signal and decoy settings.
- Vacuum probabilities implied by the other setting probabilities.

These parameters influence the detection statistics and the estimated quantities entering the key-rate calculation.

### Actively Odd-Parity Pairing

AOPP is a classical post-processing technique applied to the raw key generated by an SNS-type protocol. It uses pairing and parity-selection operations to improve the retained key's error characteristics.

Its effect cannot be represented adequately by changing the optical channel loss alone: the post-processing stage also affects the retained data and the quantities used in the final key-rate evaluation.

This repository investigates AOPP in conjunction with asymmetric channel and protocol configurations. The precise treatment of AOPP and its associated parameters should be interpreted according to the implementation in the relevant notebook section.

## System Model

### Channel Geometry

Let

- $L_A$ denote the Alice–Charlie channel length.
- $L_B$ denote the Bob–Charlie channel length.
- $alpha$ denote the fibre attenuation coefficient in dB/km.

The notebook considers both symmetric and asymmetric configurations.

A symmetric-distance configuration satisfies

$$
L_A=L_B,
$$

whereas an asymmetric-distance configuration satisfies

$$
L_A\ne L_B.
$$

For a fixed total channel length,

$$
L_{\mathrm{tot}}=L_A+L_B,
$$

the distribution of distance between the two arms can be varied while keeping the total distance constant. This makes it possible to investigate whether the key-rate maximum occurs at equal distances or at a nonzero distance asymmetry.

Equal distances and equal transmittances are not interchangeable assumptions in every possible physical setup. This distinction should be considered when comparing configurations.

### Channel Transmittance

The notebook uses the standard exponential loss model for optical-fibre transmission:

$$
\eta_A=10^{-\alpha L_A/10},
\qquad
\eta_B=10^{-\alpha L_B/10}.
$$

Here, \(\eta_A\) and \(\eta_B\) denote the modelled channel transmittances for the two arms.

These expressions describe attenuation due to fibre loss. Additional implementation-dependent parameters, such as detector characteristics and dark counts, must be considered separately where included in the model.

### Detection Gains

The notebook implements gain calculations for different combinations of coherent-state intensities and vacuum settings.

One form used in the asymmetric model is

\[
Q(\mu_A,\mu_B)
=
1-(1-Y_0)
\exp[-\eta_A\mu_A-\eta_B\mu_B],
\]

where:

- \(\mu_A,\mu_B\) are the intensities selected by Alice and Bob.
- \(Y_0\) is the background or vacuum detection contribution used in the model.
- \(\eta_A,\eta_B\) are the respective channel transmittances.
- \(Q(\mu_A,\mu_B)\) is the modelled detection gain for that intensity combination.

The gain model is used to calculate statistics for vacuum, decoy, and signal settings. The notebook contains multiple modelling stages, so the exact expression and interpretation should be checked against the specific function being evaluated.

### Decoy-State Estimation and Key-Rate Evaluation

The numerical routines calculate detection gains and use decoy-state statistics to estimate single-photon yields. The notebook also includes binary-entropy calculations, error-related quantities, and an AOPP-associated post-processing stage.

The binary entropy function is

\[
h_2(x)=-x\log_2 x-(1-x)\log_2(1-x),
\]

for \(0<x<1\), with the endpoint values defined by continuity.

These quantities enter the key-rate objective used for comparisons and optimization. The repository should not be interpreted as implementing a single universal formula for every section: the notebook contains successive versions of the model with different parameterizations and assumptions.

**Important:** The expressions above describe selected components of the implemented numerical model. They are not, by themselves, a complete finite-key security proof for SNS-TF-QKD with AOPP.

## Computational Methodology

The implementation is provided in a Jupyter notebook:

[`Asymmetric-SNS-TF-QKD-AOPP.ipynb`](Asymmetric-SNS-TF-QKD-AOPP.ipynb)

The notebook uses Python's numerical and plotting ecosystem to investigate the model.

### 1. Direct Key-Rate Evaluation

The asymmetric key-rate routines accept channel lengths and protocol parameters for Alice and Bob. They calculate the relevant channel transmittances, detection gains, estimated yields, error-related quantities, and the resulting model key-rate objective.

### 2. Distance-Asymmetry Sweeps

The notebook varies the distance distribution between Alice and Bob while examining the resulting key-rate behaviour. Comparisons include symmetric-distance configurations and selected asymmetric configurations.

### 3. Protocol-Parameter Exploration

The calculations investigate the effect of varying parameters such as:

- Decoy-state intensity \(\mu_x\).
- Weak-signal intensity \(\mu_y\).
- Strong-signal intensity \(\mu_z\).
- Signal and decoy selection probabilities.
- Channel lengths and attenuation-related parameters.

Some sections use common parameters for both users, while others allow Alice and Bob to have independent values.

### 4. Numerical Optimization

The notebook includes constrained optimization routines using SciPy's optimization tools, including SLSQP-based searches in selected sections.

The optimization variables can include signal intensities and sending probabilities. Constraints enforce selected bounds, intensity ordering, and probability normalization conditions.

Some exploratory sections also use random initial points or random parameter sampling. These methods are numerical searches; they do not automatically establish a global optimum. Results may depend on bounds, initial conditions, stopping criteria, and the selected key-rate model.

### 5. Visualization and Interactive Exploration

The notebook includes plots for examining key-rate variation with distance asymmetry and protocol parameters. It also contains interactive controls for changing selected inputs and observing the corresponding model outputs.

## Interpreting the Results

The numerical comparisons are intended to investigate the relationship between channel asymmetry, protocol-parameter choices, and the resulting key-rate objective.

The following distinctions are important:

- **Fixed parameters versus optimized parameters:** A comparison at identical or fixed protocol parameters answers a different question from a comparison in which each configuration is independently optimized.
- **Distance asymmetry versus parameter asymmetry:** Unequal channel lengths do not require unequal signal intensities or sending probabilities, and the two forms of asymmetry should be distinguished.
- **Local versus global optimization:** A numerically returned optimum is conditional on the objective, constraints, search strategy, and convergence behaviour.
- **Model output versus a security guarantee:** A positive value returned by a numerical model does not independently establish that a complete implementation satisfies all assumptions required for a composable security proof.

The notebook is exploratory and parameter-dependent. Conclusions should be drawn from the relevant calculations and assumptions rather than generalized to all asymmetric SNS-TF-QKD configurations.
